AdoptiScope™: Adoption Barrier Diagnostic

AdoptiScope is a change-management diagnostic built as a workbook. It finds where adoption breaks for each group of people a change touches, confirms it with evidence, and only then helps you choose interventions matched to the failing condition.

What it is

Find the barrier. Fit the intervention.

Diagnose first. Prescribe second.

It follows the FIRST method, five steps: Frame · Isolate · Root · Select · Track. The workbook walks a practitioner through them: name the populations a change touches, find each one's barrier point (the first link in Why → Want → How → Can → Keep that fails), confirm it before spending money on it, find the specific condition that is failing, and only then pick interventions that answer that condition. After that, re-score every population round by round until the new way is the default.

It comes as an Excel workbook and as a native Google Sheet, built from the same code, with the same formulas, views and charts. A fictional worked example, Meridian Field Services, shows every feature firing.

The barrier point is the first link that fails. Links after it can't be read reliably until it clears, so the workbook dims them.

Why we built it

Most avoidable resistance is a diagnostic problem.

Most avoidable resistance is not a people problem or a culture problem. It is a diagnostic problem: programs prescribe the most familiar intervention, usually training, before finding where the breakdown is. The Prescription Trap article makes that case; AdoptiScope is the instrument that stops it happening.

So the governing rule is enforced, not just printed on the cover: Diagnose first. Prescribe second. The shortlist of interventions stays hidden until a failing condition is marked, and every planned intervention gets a Prescription Check that names the trap when it doesn't match the population's confirmed barrier.

And there is no organization-wide score anywhere, by design. Different groups are at different barrier points at the same time; an average hides exactly the information a change leader needs.

How it is used

A first diagnosis in about 30 minutes.

A first diagnosis takes about 30 minutes: F1 Setup → F2 Populations → I1 Sweep → I2 Confirm → S1 Plan → Readiness Map. Everything else adds depth, and the workbook still works if you skip it.

Cream cells are for input; white cells are computed. Dropdowns carry the allowed values, and hovering over a column header explains it.

Then re-run the Sweep each round (baseline, pre-go-live, go-live, day 30, 60, 90 …). Change the current round in F1 Setup and every view follows it. Barriers move; regression is its own diagnosis.

The worked example

Meridian Field Services (fictional): One Way to Operate

Order-to-Operate standardized across every acquired branch: one field-service management platform, one standard work-order process from dispatch to invoice, and a mobile technician app. Today each acquired company runs its own dispatch board, its own paperwork and its own definition of "done".

The screenshots in this guide come from this example: 8 populations scored over 6 rounds (Baseline, Pre-go-live, Go-live, Day 30, Day 60, Day 90), seeded so that every flag in the workbook fires at least once. Meridian is the fictional company from the process-excellence series ↗.

The populations

  • Dispatchers and schedulers (legacy branches)
  • Senior field technicians (long-tenured, acquired firms)
  • Field technicians, night and on-call
  • Service sales and account managers (Lead-to-Order seam)
  • Branch managers
  • Parts and procurement coordinators (Source-to-Pay seam)
  • Billing and finance operations
  • New-hire technicians (joined after go-live)
