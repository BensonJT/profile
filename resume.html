Selected engagements · Verizon, 1994–2025

The capacity nobody could see

Senior Manager, Global Network Operations

2019 – 2025

A thousand person field organization was carrying overtime while skills sat idle elsewhere in the same operation, and nobody could see it, because the work area map underneath had grown without governance. It was incomplete, non-contiguous, and redundant. A single ZIP code could appear in six different areas. Dispatch automation was running on geography that did not exist.

I rebuilt the foundation from zero with the field supervisors, who carried the local knowledge no data model could replace: all 41,326 ZIP codes, contiguous, each one in exactly one area. That 1:1 constraint is what made everything downstream possible. Then I volunteered to build the thing the clean foundation now allowed, a Force-to-Load capacity model in Python and SQL that mapped skill supply against demand by geography and shift.

$18–22M
Hidden capacity surfaced, converted into a prioritized cross-training roadmap
50 FTE
Approved on the data-backed business case
+10%
Automated dispatch accuracy, from the foundation work alone

The $40M rationalization

Manager, IT Asset Management · Black Belt on the program

2013 – 2019

An enterprise expense mandate arrived with the usual reflex: freeze hiring, find headcount to cut. But in an IT asset lifecycle that had been running long enough, the waste had become invisible. Forensic decomposition found 400 distinct activities, 110 handoffs, and 64 interconnected systems.

Rather than redesigning in isolation, we co-facilitated a Work Out session that ran from the data center floor to the SVP level, so the people who would have to live in the new model helped build it. Forty three percent of the critical path turned out to exist because it had always existed, not because it added value.

Fifteen servers a week to five hundred

Manager, Enterprise Security Migration

2013 – 2019

A 30,000 server security migration was moving at fifteen servers a week. Every executive deadline was unreachable at that pace. The people were working hard; the system they were working inside was the constraint. Data lived in 27 disconnected sources, so problems surfaced during change windows instead of before them.

A DMAIC rebuild pulled 27 data sources into one view of server readiness and re-sequenced the workflow into a pull-based pre-qualify, schedule, implement order, so failures were found and fixed before the window rather than inside it.

33×
Peak deployment velocity, 15 to 500 servers per week
27
Data sources harmonized into a single operational picture
84%
Reduction in hardware holding time, asset utilization to 80%

When clarity turned out to be the culture fix

Senior Member of Technical Staff, 24×7 Technical Operations

Early career

A skilled, motivated operations team was losing half its people every year. It was not a pay problem and it was not a culture problem. Nobody had ever documented how the work was supposed to be done, so every technician was guessing their way through mission critical tasks and carrying the quiet exhaustion of never being sure they were right.

We built an ISO 9000 quality system from the ground up by walking the technicians through their actual daily work: 143 standard operating procedures, process flows, and decision trees for the edge cases, every one refined through their input, made accessible through a knowledge management system in the operating environment.
