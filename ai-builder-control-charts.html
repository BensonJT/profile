How I evaluate AI

Control charts

My job-search pipeline uses an AI judge every night. These charts show whether the judge, and the runs around it, stay steady over time. They are the same control charts a process team keeps on a production line. Rebuilt after each pipeline run; built November 12, 2026.

The limits are wider than coin-flip luck alone would give, because the share swings more than that from night to night: the mix of postings changes, and so does the judge's prompt. So the limits are set from how much the share really moves between runs (a Laney p′ chart), and a point outside them is a real surprise. 0 of 35 runs sit outside the limits. The prompt changed 13 times in this period, marked under the chart. Every run so far was answered by the same model (jev-1.13.0). The postings in my answer key are left out, so a full test does not swing the night's share.

How to read these charts

Limits, signals and the pass bar

Centre line
The average of the counted points.
Limits
How far a point can move from the centre by chance alone, set at three standard errors. On a share they widen when a point is based on fewer items.
Signal
A counted point outside the limits. Something changed, and I look for the cause before I trust the next run. "In control" means the latest counted point is inside the limits; "Last signal" gives the date of the newest point outside them.
Pass bar
The score I require before the judge may move anything on my list. It is a requirement, not a limit: a judge can be steady and still below the bar.
Hollow marks
Points shown for the record but left out of the centre line and limits: a test cut short by a spending cap or an account error is never scored, and a version of the judge that never passed its bar was never put to use.

Source: my job-search pipeline's own logs, copied to PostgreSQL after each run. The charts show rates and counts of the pipeline's behaviour only. Dates are US Eastern time.
