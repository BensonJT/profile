Control charts

Each new version of the judge's prompt starts its own center line and limits, because a new prompt is a new process: 14 versions so far. A version gets limits once it has 5 runs; 3 have reached that, and the shorter ones are drawn with no limits. The limits are wider than coin-flip luck alone would give, because the share swings more than that from night to night as the mix of postings changes. So they are set from how much the share really moves between runs (a Laney p′ chart). Signals: 1 of the runs that have limits. Every run so far was answered by the same model (jev-1.13.0). The postings in my answer key are left out, so a full test does not swing the night's share.

How to read these charts

Limits, signals and the pass bar

Center line
The average of the counted points.
Limits
How far a point can move from the center by chance alone, set at three standard errors. On a share they widen when a point is based on fewer items.
Signal
A counted point outside the limits, or a pattern that chance rarely makes. Something changed, and I look for the cause before I trust the next run. "In control" means the latest counted point gives no signal. Each status also gives the date of the latest point and of the newest signal.
Run rules
Patterns inside the limits that also count as signals: 2 of 3 points in a row beyond two standard errors on one side, 4 of 5 beyond one, or 8 in a row on one side of the center line (the Western Electric rules). I use them on the judge's tests, its nightly verdicts and the reading time. The failure charts use the limits alone: most of their points are 0, so they sit below the center by design.
Restarted limits
When the judge's prompt changes, the nightly-verdict chart starts a new center line and new limits, because a new prompt is a new process. A dotted line marks each restart.
Pass bar
The score I require before the judge may move anything on my list. It is a requirement, not a limit: a judge can be steady and still below the bar.
Hollow marks
Points shown for the record but left out of the center line and limits: a test cut short by a spending cap or an account error is never scored, and a version of the judge that never passed its bar was never put to use.

Source: my job-search pipeline's own logs, copied to PostgreSQL after each run. The charts show rates and counts of the pipeline's behaviour only. Dates are US Eastern time.
