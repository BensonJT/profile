Where I get called in

Four sentences I have heard in every industry.

“Nobody can explain the number.”

Spend keeps climbing and no one can say why. Forecasts arrive that the team quietly does not trust.

Usually the number is fine and the thing underneath it is not. On one engagement the answer was not productivity at all. It was minimum fee thresholds nobody had tracked against actual volume.

“The process only works because certain people are still here.”

Nothing is written down. It runs on memory, goodwill, and two people who have been there long enough.

That is not a talent problem, it is an ambiguity problem. One operations group went from fifty percent annual attrition to one percent, and the only thing that changed was that the work was finally written down.

“The team is working harder and the throughput will not move.”

More effort in, the same amount out. Everyone is tired and the number is flat.

When effort rises and output does not, the constraint is almost never the people. One migration sat at fifteen servers a week. Same team, redesigned flow, five hundred a week at peak.

“We are paying for capacity we cannot see.”

Overtime climbing while skills sit idle somewhere else in the same organization.

Capacity that is not modeled is capacity you buy twice. One field organization was carrying eighteen to twenty two million dollars of it, distributed invisibly across a thousand people.

The method

How I see a process.

Every process comes apart the same way. Start with the whole thing as one box. Break it into the three to six activities that actually perform it. Break those down again. Keep going until each activity is small enough to assign, measure, and improve.

Bake BrowniesA0
Acquire IngredientsA1
Set-up EnvironmentA2
Mix IngredientsA3
Check the pantryA1.1
Buy what is missingA1.2
Verify the receiptA1.3

At every level, four questions: who supplies the input, what governs how it is done, what comes out, and who receives it. That is it. It works on a bakery and it works on a two point two billion dollar spend portfolio. I have used it on both.
