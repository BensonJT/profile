Where I get pulled in

Four sentences I have heard in every domain.

“Nobody can explain the number.”

Spend keeps climbing and no one can say why. Forecasts arrive that the team quietly does not trust.

Usually the number is fine and the thing underneath it is not. On one engagement the answer was not productivity at all. It was minimum fee thresholds nobody had tracked against actual volume.

“The process only works because certain people are still here.”

Nothing is written down. It runs on memory, goodwill, and two people who have been there long enough.

One operations group went from 50% annual attrition to 1%, and the only thing that changed was that the work was finally written down.

“The team is working harder and the throughput will not move.”

More effort in, the same amount out. Everyone is tired and the number is flat.

When effort rises and output does not, the constraint is almost never the people. One migration sat at 15 servers a week. Same team, redesigned flow, 500 a week at peak.

“We are paying for capacity we cannot see.”

Overtime climbing while skills sit idle somewhere else in the same organization.

One field organization had $18–22M of capacity it could not see, spread across 1,000 people.

The method

How I see a process.

Every process comes apart the same way. Start with the whole thing as one box. Break it into the 3 to 6 activities that perform it. Break those down again. Keep going until each activity is small enough to assign, measure, and improve.

Bake BrowniesA0
Acquire IngredientsA1
Check the pantryA1.1
Buy what is missingA1.2
Verify the receiptA1.3
Set-up EnvironmentA2
Preheat the ovenA2.1
Line the baking dishA2.2
Lay out the toolsA2.3
Mix IngredientsA3
Combine dry ingredientsA3.1
Combine wet ingredientsA3.2
Fold into batterA3.3
Bake BrowniesA4
Load the ovenA4.1
Hold at temperatureA4.2
Test for donenessA4.3
Serve BrowniesA5
Cool and cutA5.1
Plate the servingsA5.2
Serve the customerA5.3
Clean-up KitchenA6
Clear the dishesA6.1
Wash and dryA6.2
Reset the workspaceA6.3

At every level, four questions: who supplies the input, what governs how it is done, what comes out, and who receives it. That is it. It works on a bakery and it works on a $2.2B spend portfolio. I have used it on both.
